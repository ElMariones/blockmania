# BLOCKMANIA launch marketing plan

**Working date:** 2026-09-25. **Target:** late October 2026, subject to Steam review, a public Coming Soon page, and launch quality. **Owner:** solo developer. **Confirmed by owner:** store page not yet public; **0 wishlists**, intended price **€7–12**, marketing cash **about €100**, and **not registered** for October Steam Next Fest. Exact release day remains open.

## Executive decision

Sell one clear transformation: **an 8×8 block puzzle becomes a buildcraft score machine**. A recognizable placement makes a row and column vanish; a built bag and ordered Jokers turn that simple move into an absurd, fully explained score. The audience is primarily PC roguelike/buildcraft players who enjoy finding combinations, secondarily puzzle players who want a readable board and low-pressure play. The pitch should lead with what happens in play, then show the volume of content.

The current assets already include original capsules, screenshots, localized EN/ES/ZH store text, gameplay clips, and a 72-second trailer. A new large asset campaign would waste the short runway. Re-cut those assets into short, real-game proof clips. Verify that any final marketing claim matches the release build. The game UI is presently English only; localized store copy is not a claim of localized game UI. Do not claim full controller or Steam Deck support yet.

**Recommended late-October date:** October 29 or 30 if the page is public by October 10, the build passes review and external playtests, and the first media make the hook obvious. If the page is still in review on October 15, or the build is unstable, move the date rather than release into a broken first impression. Steam requires a public Coming Soon page for at least two weeks, and recommends submitting store presence at least seven days before it must go live. [Coming Soon](https://partner.steamgames.com/doc/store/coming_soon), [Release Process](https://partner.steamgames.com/doc/store/releasing).

**Commercial choice:** Late October is a feasible shipping plan, but four weeks of marketing from zero wishlists and €100 cannot responsibly be forecast as a breakout. If the owner's overriding goal is to maximize the chance of a larger launch, the stronger option is to keep the page public, build an audience and demo through winter, register for the **February 22–March 1, 2027** Next Fest by its published **January 10** deadline, and release afterward if eligible. This trades a roughly four-month delay for time to learn which hook brings qualified players. [February 2027 Next Fest](https://partner.steamgames.com/doc/marketing/upcoming_events/nextfest/feb_2027).

## October event status

October Steam Next Fest runs **October 19–26**. Its registration deadline was **August 31**. The owner confirms BLOCKMANIA was **not registered**, so the late-October plan excludes festival placement. A game under store review is not by itself registered for the festival. [October 2026 Next Fest rules](https://partner.steamgames.com/doc/marketing/upcoming_events/nextfest/2026october?l=english).

| Route | Action |
|---|---|
| Late October (owner's target) | Do not advertise Next Fest participation. A standalone demo can still give creators something to play, but prioritize a solid Coming Soon page, creator previews, and the full launch. |
| Audience-building alternative | Prepare a polished demo for a later event, including February 2027 Next Fest if eligible, and use the extra months for creator play and message testing. |

**Notification trap:** a standalone demo can send one wishlist notification manually, but Steam imposes a roughly two-week notification cooldown for the same app. If releasing any demo within two weeks before an October 29/30 full launch, **do not trigger the optional demo wishlist notification**; preserve the launch notification. The demo itself can still be public and promoted externally. Confirm the latest rule in Steamworks before scheduling. [Demo notification rules](https://partner.steamgames.com/doc/store/application/demos?l=english), [Wishlist notifications](https://partner.steamgames.com/doc/marketing/wishlist?l=english).

## Positioning and store conversion

**One-line pitch:** “Build a bag of blocks, bend the rules with Jokers, and turn an 8×8 line clear into a ridiculous score.”

**Proof in the first 10 seconds:** (1) three-piece tray and readable 8×8 board, (2) a move that clears row + column, (3) Joker triggers and the score receipt, (4) a huge score or boss rule. Real gameplay should be prominent; an animated title sequence cannot replace a player seeing the loop. Put a gameplay-first cut before or alongside the existing stylized 72-second trailer. Make the first 4–5 screenshots tell place → clear → Joker/shop → boss → progression. Use screenshots from the shipping build. The useful lesson from breakout examples such as Balatro is to make a distinctive combination *demonstrable* in seconds; their sales are exceptional outcomes, and Balatro also had a publisher, Playstack. [Balatro's Steam listing](https://store.steampowered.com/app/2379780/Balatro/).

**Messaging ladder:**

1. **Immediate hook:** “One block. Two lines. A million points.” (Use only a captured real-game result.)
2. **Build fantasy:** The bag itself changes: materials, copies, rotations, removals, and upgrades; Joker order changes the calculation.
3. **Credibility:** Score preview and itemized receipt explain each result; 12 rounds and bosses give the run a destination.
4. **Depth for the curious:** five Kits, Heat, Daily, Endless, Overtime, achievements and finishes. These sell depth after the hook, not before it.

**Store page QA after approval:** view the live page on desktop and mobile; check tiny capsule legibility, first trailer frame, screenshot order, tags, supported language claims, and demo button. Invite five target players who have not built the game to answer within ten seconds: “What do you do, why is it different, and what is the next action?” If fewer than four can answer, fix the first media and short description before spending effort on reach. Valve says tags affect categorization and visibility; wishlists mainly create launch notification and Popular Upcoming opportunities, while purchases and play are stronger visibility signals. Traffic and store conversion rate are not direct ranking inputs. [Visibility on Steam](https://partner.steamgames.com/doc/marketing/visibility).

## Audience and channel priority

| Priority | Audience / channel | Why it fits | Solo-developer execution |
|---|---|---|---|
| 1 | Small and medium creators who regularly play roguelikes, deckbuilders, score chasers, and puzzle strategy | They can demonstrate a surprising build, which explains the game better than a static ad | Build a researched list of 40–60; send 10–15 personal notes each week; track reply, key/demo acceptance, coverage, and wishlists through unique links. No paid coverage assumption. |
| 2 | Steam page, demo, Steam events/news | Every outside mention needs a working place to wishlist or buy | Publish Coming Soon ASAP; one focused demo/launch event, one patch/feedback post after launch. Reply to substantive comments daily around launch. |
| 3 | Short gameplay video: YouTube Shorts, TikTok, Reels, plus one community where gameplay clips are welcome | A single score surprise is legible without explaining all 70 Jokers | Two distinct clips per week, adapted from the same captured session; captions and a clear Steam call to action. Follow community rules and participate as a player, not a link dropper. |
| 4 | Niche press / newsletters / curators | Useful for credibility if the pitch is a specific story | Send 10–15 relevant outlets or newsletters a concise personal pitch with a press kit. Curator Connect can deliver pre-release copies to relevant Steam curators, but none are obliged to cover it. [Curator Connect](https://partner.steamgames.com/doc/marketing/curators). |
| Later | Broad paid ads, a large Discord, daily livestreams | Expensive in attention or cash before the organic message is proven | Run only a small tracked experiment after the page converts qualified traffic and the launch build is stable. |

**Creator qualification:** recent videos on comparable *mechanics* (buildcraft or score optimization), real audience interaction, clear contact method, and a format that allows 15–30 minutes of play. Do not buy unverified “influencer packages.” Send a playable demo or preview copy and allow honest coverage. Suggested subject: `A block puzzle roguelike where you upgrade the pieces themselves — BLOCKMANIA`. Suggested note: “I’m the solo developer of BLOCKMANIA. Your [specific recent video] made me think you might enjoy the bag-building decisions: a normal row/column clear can become an absurd Joker chain, with every point itemized. Here is a 20-second real-play clip, a Steam page, and a demo/preview copy if useful. Release is planned for late October.” One follow-up after 5–7 days, then stop.

**Time budget:** target 6–8 marketing hours/week before launch: 2 h capture/edit, 2 h personalized outreach, 1 h community replies, 1 h analytics, 1–2 h store/press materials. During release week, reserve 1–2 h/day for support and outreach. Cut channels before cutting QA or sleep.

## Measurement, experiments, and forecasts

Create Steam UTM links such as `https://store.steampowered.com/app/5328810/?utm_source=youtube&utm_medium=creator&utm_campaign=launch_2026&utm_content=creatorname`. Use separate links for each creator and each owned channel. Steam reports tracked signed-in visits and wishlist/purchase conversions within **72 hours**; totals differ from all traffic because of sign-in and privacy settings. Read these as directional attribution, not a complete census. [UTM Analytics](https://partner.steamgames.com/doc/marketing/utm_analytics).

Record every Monday: cumulative wishlists, net new wishlists/week, Steam store impressions and visits, qualified UTM visits, UTM wishlist rate, demo downloads and play/completion (if available), outreach sent/replies/coverage, bugs in first 20 minutes, and after launch units, refunds, review themes. Note release or creator dates on the same chart. A “successful” viral view count with no Steam activity is not a successful campaign.

**Working experiment gates, not universal industry benchmarks:** after at least 200 tracked, relevant Steam visits to one creative, if fewer than 5% wishlist, inspect page/media/audience fit; if above 10%, repeat that angle. Compare sources against each other and against the same page over time. If low clicks, change the opening frame or targeting. If clicks but weak wishlist rate, change the first gameplay proof, short description, or page claims. If wishlists arrive but demo players leave early, fix onboarding and clarity before wider outreach.

With zero current wishlists, the October forecast is driven by the next four weeks of page traffic and creator response. **Recommended price for planning:** €9.99 list, with an optional 10% launch discount (about €8.99). This sits inside the owner's €7–12 range; validate it against actual playtest reactions and final polish, and do not lower price to compensate for weak awareness. The table is an **illustrative sensitivity model**, *not* a claim about Steam's average conversion. It assumes a hypothetical 5%, 10%, or 20% of prelaunch wishlisters buy in the first month. It excludes people who discover the game after launch and says nothing about refunds, VAT, Steam's share, or developer take-home.

| Wishlists at launch | 5% case | 10% case | 20% case |
|---:|---:|---:|---:|
| 100 | 5 units / ~€45 gross | 10 / ~€90 | 20 / ~€180 |
| 500 | 25 / ~€225 | 50 / ~€450 | 100 / ~€899 |
| 2,000 | 100 / ~€899 | 200 / ~€1,798 | 400 / ~€3,596 |

For **prelaunch wishlist acquisition**, use a separate funnel: relevant external views × Steam click rate × wishlist rate. Example: 40,000 relevant clip views × 1% click × 10% wishlist = **40 wishlists**. A small creator who provides another 10,000 relevant views under the same assumptions adds **10**. These percentages are planning assumptions, not observed BLOCKMANIA data. If the clips only reach 4,000 people, this math predicts about four wishlists; a strong creator placement could exceed all these cases. Replace the assumptions with real UTM rates after the first 200+ tracked visits. A Balatro-scale breakout is an upside event, not a responsible base case: this plan maximizes the chances of a clear first impression, creator demonstration, and good launch experience, then lets actual market response determine how aggressively to extend the campaign.

## Cash allocation and no-go list

The cash budget is **about €100**. Use **€0 on ads** this launch cycle. Reserve up to €60 to compensate three external target-player testers (€20 each) if free testers are unavailable; reserve €40 for a concrete page/trailer issue revealed by testing, such as captions or copy proofing. If neither is needed, keep the cash. Do not buy followers, wishlist services, vague press distribution, or broad ads against an untested store page. Do not trade features or false localization claims for marketing reach.
